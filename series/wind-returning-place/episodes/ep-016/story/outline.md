# 바람이 돌아오는 곳 16: 돌아가는 쟁반

## Story lock

Doyun notices a scratched blue enamel tray in the Seoul gallery tea corner and thinks its visible wear should be protected behind a frame. A photo from Soondeok takes him to a Jeju harbor meal where the same kind of tray keeps moving between kitchen, table, and wash basin. His goal is to decide whether the tray is an archive object or a working object. The concrete problem is that removing it from the meal would leave other people carrying cups and bowls separately. He chooses circulation over preservation and makes a visible return loop in Seoul.

Minjae is absent in the present. Yunseo remains an elementary-school-age child with a direct question and her own decision. Soondeok offers practical friction rather than a lesson. This episode avoids recent access, empty-chair, waiting-room, label, drawer, and gap structures: its carrier is a circulating work tray, its setting rhythm is a harbor meal, and its payoff is a completed return loop.

## Twenty concrete beats

1. In Seoul, Doyun notices blue enamel scratches on a tray beneath used tea cups.
2. He lifts the tray away from visitors, thinking the chip should not be used again.
3. Soondeok sends a photo of a matching tray at a Jeju harbor meal.
4. Doyun arrives at a low harbor restaurant before the lunch rush.
5. The chipped tray carries bowls from the kitchen to a long table.
6. He asks if it should be put aside before the chip grows worse.
7. Soondeok puts two bowls on it and tells him to carry it carefully, not hide it.
8. Doyun carries the tray through the doorway, learning its weight changes with what it holds.
9. Yunseo finishes her soup and looks for where her bowl should go.
10. She asks whether the tray is going back to the kitchen.
11. A tired fisherwoman arrives with cups in both hands and cannot open the wash-room door.
12. Yunseo puts her empty bowl on the tray and moves it toward the door herself.
13. Doyun takes the other side only after Yunseo has started, so the fisherwoman can pass.
14. The tray returns to the kitchen with bowls, cups, and its old chip still visible.
15. Doyun photographs the tray from above after it is washed, not anyone's face.
16. On the ferry home, he deletes a draft label that called the tray a keepsake.
17. In Seoul, he places a plain blue return tray beside the gallery tea cups.
18. He leaves it low and clear, with no instruction except the visible stacked cups.
19. A visitor finishes tea and places the cup on the tray; another visitor carries it to the counter.
20. At closing, Doyun sees the clean tray ready for morning: useful things can return by being passed on.
