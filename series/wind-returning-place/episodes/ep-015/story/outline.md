# 바람이 돌아오는 곳 15: 남겨 둔 의자

## Story lock

Doyun sees an empty chair in his Seoul gallery and learns from a Jeju harbor waiting room that a chair can remain for a tired person without being reserved for a particular absence. His goal is to decide whether to label and preserve the chair or leave it plainly usable. The problem is concrete: he keeps trying to assign the seat to a story, while Soondeok and Yunseo use it as ordinary furniture. He chooses availability over memorialization and makes the resulting change visible in Seoul.

Minjae is absent in the present. Yunseo stays a child with her own small decision. Soondeok provides practical resistance rather than a lesson. The motif differs from recent warmth, access, wall-gap, and waiting narratives: it is a worn public waiting-room chair, the carrier is a physical seat rather than a clue, and the payoff is a visitor resting and moving on.

## Twenty concrete beats

1. In Seoul, Doyun notices an empty wooden chair beside his gallery wall.
2. He moves a stack of prints off it, thinking it should not look abandoned.
3. Soondeok sends him a photo of the same kind of chair at the Jeju harbor waiting room.
4. Doyun arrives at the windy waiting room with his camera and the photo.
5. The chair is scratched but dry, facing the ferry timetable without any name attached.
6. He starts to tape a small card to its back so people will know why it matters.
7. Soondeok removes the tape and says the chair matters only when someone can sit.
8. Doyun leaves the chair bare but stays nearby, unsure whether that is careless.
9. Yunseo returns from the pier, tired from carrying her small bag, and sits down without asking.
10. She drinks water, points at the ferry, and says she will stand when the next person needs it.
11. A wet-haired delivery worker arrives, sees the chair occupied, and leans against the wall instead.
12. Yunseo notices him and gets up on her own, taking her bag but leaving the water cup at the bin.
13. The worker sits only long enough to retie a shoe and then hurries to the boat.
14. Doyun photographs the chair after each person leaves, not their faces.
15. He realizes the chair has not become empty; it has become ready again.
16. On the ferry home, he stops writing a plaque and folds the blank card into his camera case.
17. In Seoul, Doyun sands and repairs a plain gallery chair without adding a dedication.
18. He places it by the window with a coat hook and a small cup of water nearby.
19. A visitor rests there while waiting for rain to pass; Doyun records only the visitor's shoes and the chair legs.
20. At closing, Doyun leaves the chair in place and the blank card in his pocket: room can be left without being assigned.
